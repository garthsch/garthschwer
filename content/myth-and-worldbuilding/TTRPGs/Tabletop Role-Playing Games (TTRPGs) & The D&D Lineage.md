# Tabletop Role-Playing Games (TTRPGs) & The D&D Lineage

Tabletop Role-Playing Games (TTRPGs) are a distinct medium of game design centered on **collaborative storytelling**. Unlike traditional board games with fixed paths or victory conditions, TTRPGs rely on a conversation between a facilitator (often called the Game Master or Dungeon Master) and the players, where outcomes are determined by a combination of choice, rules, and chance.

### The Mechanics of Agency
The defining characteristic of the TTRPG is player agency. In video games, a player can only interact with objects the developers have coded; in a TTRPG, a player can attempt any action that makes logical sense within the fictional world. This freedom is managed through specific mechanics:

1.  **The Conversation:** The loop of play usually follows a pattern where the GM describes a situation, the players describe their response, and the dice determine the success or failure of that response.
2.  **The Social Contract:** Because the game exists in the collective imagination (often called "Theatre of the Mind"), it requires a tacit agreement on tone, rules, and boundaries—often established in a [[Session Zero]].
3.  **Randomness:** Dice (or cards/tokens) serve as the arbiter of uncertainty, introducing tension and preventing the story from becoming purely arbitrary.

### Dungeons & Dragons: The Progenitor
While role-playing existed in improvisational theater and psychological exercises previously, **Dungeons & Dragons (D&D)** formalized the genre in 1974. Created by Gary Gygax and Dave Arneson, it evolved from miniature wargaming (specifically a set of rules called *Chainmail*).

D&D shifted the scope of play from commanding mass military units to controlling a single avatar. This shift created the "adventure party" dynamic that dominates the genre today. While D&D remains the market leader—bolstered significantly by the 5th Edition and the rise of "Actual Play" media like *Critical Role*—it is merely one system within a vast ecosystem. Other systems often diverge from D&D's combat-heavy roots to focus on investigation, political intrigue, or narrative-first gameplay.
